The S8 full true generating law beats the fixed true-backbone null by 16.43 nats, the intercept-refitted true backbone by 14.54 and the jointly fitted null by 18.11; partial oracles recover 3.13 nats (intercept-only), 2.824 nats (multi-start five-parameter refit) and a -6.513-nat loss when the true backbone is combined with each history's fitted attenuation, so estimation loss of 17.82 nats erases about 98% of the oracle advantage. How to read this comparison

What does a score mean?

A log score rewards a model for assigning more probability to what happened, while accounting for the activity it predicted during quiet periods. A higher score is better. A nat is a natural-log scoring unit - not a percentage of earthquakes predicted correctly.

Joint scores combine timing, location and magnitude. Timing concerns when activity occurs, including quiet periods. Location concerns where events occur conditional on their times. In the spatial study the magnitude contribution is shared, so its difference cancels.

Model names, uncertainty and provenance

OC uses Omori time decay with background-weighted continuous spatial triggering. EP uses an unweighted inverse-power spatial triggering model. FC uses a fixed-scale Mittag-Leffler time law; MC estimates a common scale. LI attenuates the background-only rate with equal event weights. LI+ uses magnitude-weighted inhibition, applied either to the background alone or to the total rate. The unweighted arm removes background weighting from the offspring kernel; LOO changes background estimation to leave-one-out.

For observed-window comparisons, these windows are dependent and were explored during research development. The observed selectors show point contrasts. Paired 90-, 180- and 365-day block summaries are reported in the current guide and monograph. Long-memory dependence and exchangeability are not established; intervals are descriptive sensitivity summaries and sign-flip references are nominal, not calibrated tests. Compare block lengths, fold consistency and score components. Individual losses remain visible. For observed-window comparisons only, a pooled score is the sum of window scores; per-target pooling divides by the total target count, not an average of window averages.
