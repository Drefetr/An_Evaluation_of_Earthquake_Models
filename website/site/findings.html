How to read this comparison

What does a score mean?

A log score rewards a model for assigning more probability to what happened, while accounting for the activity it predicted during quiet periods. A higher score is better. A nat is a natural-log scoring unit - not a percentage of earthquakes predicted correctly.

Joint scores combine timing, location and magnitude. Timing concerns when activity occurs, including quiet periods. Location concerns where events occur conditional on their times. In the spatial study the magnitude contribution is shared, so its difference cancels.

Model names, uncertainty and provenance

OC uses Omori time decay with background-weighted continuous spatial triggering. EP uses an unweighted inverse-power spatial triggering model. FC uses a fixed-scale Mittag-Leffler time law; MC estimates a common scale. LI attenuates the background-only rate with equal event weights. LI+ uses magnitude-weighted inhibition, applied either to the background alone or to the total rate. The unweighted arm removes background weighting from the offspring kernel; LOO changes background estimation to leave-one-out.

For observed-window comparisons, these windows are dependent and were explored during research development. No event-independent confidence intervals or significance claims are inferred here. Individual losses remain visible. For observed-window comparisons only, a pooled score is the sum of window scores; per-target pooling divides by the total target count, not an average of window averages.
