## C1: narrow/short immigrant thinning and coupling

The reviewer's S4 counts are correct: 2,966 accepted immigrants, 1,120 accepted offspring, one rejected immigrant (seed410412), and no rejected offspring. Nineteen histories have no thinning rejection. These totals cover the entire saved 760-day histories, including warmup; they are not test-only counts. S6, the narrow/short total-intensity generator, is materially different: it rejects one immigrant and 134 offspring, and every history has at least one rejection.

Zero rejections alone would not establish same-seed equality for an arbitrary simulator. Here the code supplies the additional coupling argument. `source/generator.py:302` seeds eight separate PCG64DXSM streams from `[seed,stream_code]`. Immigrant times, locations, marks and acceptance uniforms have separate streams; offspring scheduling/Poisson and delays use stream4, locations stream5 and marks stream6. At lines578–588 the immigrant acceptance uniform is consumed even when eta is zero. Stress evaluation consumes no random draws. Offspring under the immigrant-only law are not thinned. Therefore, conditional on the same runtime, seed and all other settings and successful execution, induction over the chronological candidate queue gives identical accepted event rows for an S4 zero-rejection history and the counterfactual law with eta set to zero. Accepted parents and their child scheduling remain identical at every step.

This is a source-level coupling implication, not an executed counterfactual byte comparison. It does not equate the two probability laws, estimate total variation, or establish power from a bank of twenty histories. It does show that these particular S4 realizations offer very weak realized separation from the uninhibited process. The paper should report that fact instead of treating the label “positive control” as evidence of a strong realized signal. The source's conditional eta branch for total-intensity offspring consumes only isolated stream7; that distinction is irrelevant to the observed S4 paths but must not be silently ignored in a general coupling claim.

The proposed `protocol.json` adds one bounded intercept-only fit to each saved training history, with all other coordinates fixed, then freezes it for test. Writing b=exp(a), the objective is globally concave in b: sum log(b*u_i+v_i)-b*A-H+M. Its derivative is monotone, so endpoint checks and one bracketed scalar root suffice. Fixed spatial features need be computed only once per phase, without inhibition area-time quadrature. The largest history has296 events, and the largest phase event-parent array has39,072 entries. The data are small enough for a bounded scalar comparison; actual timing and memory remain pilot gates.

This control retains each original inhibited fit and original gate. It reports the original gain, the refitted-floor score change, and the resulting gain against that floor, with point, signed exposure and mark decomposition. It must not be described as joint nuisance estimation, a new inhibition search or a reselected complexity gate. All80 historical train/test floor comparisons must pass before completion. The exact first seed in each regime is the pilot roster; no seed substitution is permitted.

## C4: boundaries, eta-zero starts and public parameter evidence

The supplied240 fit records support the reported S4 matched-fit medians: eta2.2691404959, ell19.1478835752 and psi1.6447290964. The matched immigrant model has14/20 selected fits on at least one parameter boundary. Original unit-mark LI has17 active selected boundary fits, plus one eta-zero selected fit, so “17/20” is correct for active boundary fits and “18/20” is correct if eta=0 is itself counted as a boundary. The census reports both definitions. Exact native-coordinate equality and an absolute1e-12 tolerance in `(eta,logell,logpsi)` give the same counts; this tolerance is recorded, not chosen after examining gains.

The seed410401 LI example is accurately quoted but does not establish a broken optimizer. Start1 begins at eta0.1, ell5 and psi1/24 and reaches eta0 in two iterations/four evaluations. Its saved fine-grid negative-log-likelihood derivative in eta is positive0.027127438925255945, while the logell/logpsi derivatives are zero. The projected-gradient condition at this constrained boundary is therefore appropriate locally. At eta0, the likelihood is exactly independent of ell and psi; their zero gradients follow directly from the intensity and `cpu_reference.cpp:64–74`. A gradient optimizer can remain on this nonidentified null face even though another shape reaches a lower objective.

Start0 reaches eta1.1214933134, ell6.7467 and psi19.2935, with NLL879.528152008 rather than floor880.862695751. The multi-start rule selects this better eligible result. This demonstrates start dependence and the value of the specified multi-start procedure; it neither establishes global optimality nor proves algorithmic failure. Moreover, this example is the **unit-mark LI candidate** on a magnitude-scaled generating law: its ell/psi match does not make the starting vector the true full generating law, and eta0.1 differs from generating eta0.5. Boundary concentration and non-recovery deserve explicit descriptive reporting, but a new optimizer-failure claim requires stronger evidence.

The current paragraph at `paper/source/chapters/13_s04_expanded.tex:229` needs updating. The old compact audit authority exports score summaries and off-floor counts, but the present public package also supplies all240 fit records and selected vectors under `reproduction/synthetic/s04b/`. Describing the public evidence as if parameter distributions cannot be inspected is obsolete. “Adjust shared coordinates” is also misleading in this fixed-backbone experiment: shared background/excitation coordinates were not estimated. Suggested substance: active selection does not imply recovery; public fitted vectors show boundary concentration and start dependence, while eta-zero makes the stress-shape coordinates unidentifiable. Any new displayed numerical census requires new claim bindings by the conductor.
