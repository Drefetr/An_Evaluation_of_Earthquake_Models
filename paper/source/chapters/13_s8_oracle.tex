\subsubsection{Oracle decomposition and estimation loss}\label{sec:s8-oracle}
The oracle contrasts separate the exact generating attenuation effect from nuisance adaptation. The true generating law $\theta_\star$ evaluated against the fixed true-backbone null $\theta_{\star,0}$ gains 16.43~nats on average (median 12.82, MCSE 1.83, 20/20 wins); this is the exact generating attenuation effect. Against an intercept-refitted true backbone the mean is 14.54~nats (median 11.63, MCSE 1.83, 20/20 wins). Against the jointly fitted five-parameter null $\hat\theta_0(H_{\rm train})$ the mean is 18.11~nats (median 12.86, MCSE 4.18, 19/20 wins). The last contrast exceeds 14.54~nats by 3.57~nats because the uninhibited comparator itself gains from finite-sample estimation of $(d,n,c,p)$: on the test window, $\ell(\hat a,\theta_{\star,0,-a})-\ell(\hat\theta_0)$ averages $+3.571$~nats, while the direct true-null comparison $\ell(\theta_{\star,0})-\ell(\hat\theta_0)$ averages $+1.683$~nats. The identity closes exactly: $14.541+3.571=18.112$.

Two partial oracles locate the loss. Fixing the true attenuation $(\eta_\star,\ell_\star,\psi_\star)=(0.4,15~{\rm km},5~{\rm d})$ on each history's jointly fitted training null backbone and refitting only the scalar intercept yields a mean held-out gain of 3.13~nats (median 3.37, MCSE 1.41, 13/20 wins). Fixing the same attenuation and re-optimizing the five nuisance-backbone parameters $(a,d,n,c,p)$ on the training window yields 2.58~nats (median 5.90, MCSE 3.67, 13/20 wins). Both are far below the 18.11-nat full-information ceiling, so the dominant loss is estimation of the nuisance backbone, not absence of a generating effect. The fitted procedure captures only 0.294~nats on average; its mean estimation loss relative to $\theta_\star$ is $-17.82$~nats (median $-8.10$, MCSE $6.08$), which is $98.4\%$ of the mean oracle advantage of 18.112~nats. The fitted attenuation itself is badly estimated: $\hat\eta$ has mean 0.929, median 0.543, interquartile range $[0.264,0.984]$, minimum 0.042, maximum 4.000 and RMSE 1.230 relative to $\eta_\star=0.4$. On these 95-event histories, finite-sample estimation loss therefore erases almost all of the oracle advantage.
