\subsection{Trend--depletion controls across the New Zealand archive}\label{sec:nz-trend-controls}
The first fold accounts for 16.518~nats of the 17.623~nat shared-attenuation deficit. Across all three folds, the archived free-trend total is $-17.623$~nats. A matched zero-trend fit reduces that three-fold deficit to $-11.431$~nats (Fold~1: $-8.504$), and freezing the uninhibited trend reduces it to $-5.006$~nats; Fold~1's corresponding loss is 4.556~nats. Relative to the free fit, the zero-trend and fixed-null controls remove 48.5\% and 72.4\% of the first-fold deficit, leaving a detrended first-fold residual of 8.504~nats. The positive fitted trend, not depletion alone, accounts for most of the original first-fold loss.

The fixed-null contrast must be compared with its \emph{own} block standard error, not with the free-trend 90-day minimum detectable effect. Freezing $d=d_{\rm null}$ shrinks the fitted attenuation amplitude (Fold~0: $\hat\eta$ falls from $1.647$ to $0.178$) and with it the block-score variance. Rebuilding the paired calendar-block scores from the exact 90-, 180- and 365-day edges gives $\mathrm{SE}_{90}=1.396$~nats and $\mathrm{MDE}_{95}=2.735$~nats for the fixed-null arm, so the $-5.006$-nat three-fold loss is $-3.59$ of its own standard errors and $1.83$ times its own 90-day MDE; the corresponding values are $2.786$~nats at 180 days and $3.038$~nats at 365 days. The zero-trend arm has $\mathrm{SE}_{90}=2.764$~nats, $\mathrm{MDE}_{95}=5.417$~nats and $z=-4.14$, while the free-trend arm reproduces $\mathrm{SE}_{90}=4.712$~nats, $\mathrm{MDE}_{95}=9.236$~nats and $z=-3.74$ (Table~\ref{tab:nz-own-mde}). The smaller detrended loss therefore remains above its own contrast-specific noise scale at all three block lengths. It is not evidence of a resolved physical depletion signal, because the trend control is itself a fitted nuisance decision and the blocks are dependent; the point is only that the earlier ``below the free-trend MDE'' comparison used the wrong noise scale.

A complete eighteen-cell $(\alpha,\tau,r)$ two-start search, reproducing the ML branch of the archived adequacy protocol with an exact $\eta=0$ floor, selected the same winners as the single-start fixed-kernel controls: $(\alpha,\tau,r)=(0.5,1,0.4)$ for both the zero-trend and fixed-null arms. The full-grid held-out totals are $-8.504$ and $-4.556$~nats, identical to the reported controls at displayed precision. The full grid therefore supports, rather than overturns, the fixed-kernel arithmetic; it removes the single-start shortcut as an explanation for the detrending result.

The clamped-clock counterfactual is not a refit. It produces a Fold~1 signed contrast of $-24.791$~nats (a loss of 24.791~nats) and a three-fold total of $-26.898$~nats, close to the sum of one Fold~1 and two near-zero folds. It is reported only to show that removing positive extrapolation without re-estimating the intercept is not an admissible control. The middle fold's zero-trend fit selects the zero boundary and is tied at displayed precision, while the late fold retains a small positive strength. These two folds do not support the same attribution as the first fold. The autonomous zero-trend comparison in Table~\ref{tab:autonomous-nz} remains a separate fitted procedure with a different nuisance treatment.
