## A2 — Diagnostic scope and supported headlines

The title page now names diagnostic exponential rate attenuation in spatial–temporal earthquake models. It gives repository correspondence, keywords and an explicit AI-generation/human-responsibility statement. The title, abstract, guide and site distinguish these diagnostic models from physical Coulomb, receiver-fault and rate-and-state stress-shadow models. Internal automated checks are identified as computational or saved-output artifact audits.

The abstract and conclusion state that the comparisons establish no statistically detectable held-out improvement against the jointly fitted excitation comparators. This is a statement about the evidence, not a calibrated exclusion or a power demonstration. Selecting the nested training boundary `eta=0` produces the exact zero held-out contrasts algebraically; it does not prove zero physical inhibition. Small California total-intensity differences are described as near ties. Failed conditional calibration of both archived spatial procedures is stated beside their comparative-score interpretation.

The New Zealand block-interval statement is corrected: Fold 2 includes zero at every evaluated block length; Fold 3 includes zero at 90 days and excludes it at 180 and 365 days; Fold 1 excludes zero at all three. Fold 1 supplies 93.7% of the pooled deficit. The reported trend/depletion correlation is an event-weighted diagnostic correlation, not a full likelihood-Hessian correlation. The discussion retains the actual non-monotone depletion and signed exposure accounting rather than reviving the rejected monotone-stress explanation.

The EP+ comparison explicitly combines four changes: full-plane normalization with regional leakage, training-declustered background, magnitude-dependent spatial scales and joint fitting. All three fitted baseline spatial scales reach the 2 km lower search bound. These procedures still have valid scores on the same observed space–time domain: both intensities use the same measure, and each compensator integrates its own intensity over that domain. The improvement cannot identify which procedural change caused it. `training_power=14` denotes Sobol resolution, not statistical power.
